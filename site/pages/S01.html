S01 Documentation that tells you when it stopped being true

Holdrim keeps the documentation of a system honest. Every sentence is approved by the person who owns it, and the moment something it rests on changes, it says so — before anyone builds on it.

1 Why

Documentation rarely dies of neglect. It dies because nothing tells anyone it went stale: the rule changed and the page did not know, the screen changed and the diagram did not know. After a few months nobody can tell which parts are still true, and decisions go back to living in the heads of whoever was there.

2 Mission

The approved document is the system's source of truth: the people who know the business write it and approve it, an AI agent facilitates the building, and the code stays in view of anyone who wants to validate it.

3 Vision

Technical and non-technical people alike create, maintain and refactor complex systems starting from their business rules. The reviewed and approved documentation comes before any line of code or any screen, so nobody redoes work for want of a rule — neither the people nor an agent spending tokens on a guess.

Systems described this way are not islands. A shop's till and its supplier's sales system, both written this way, keep their contract in the same shape — so connecting them is reading two documents that already agree.
